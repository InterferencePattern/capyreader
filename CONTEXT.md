# Capy Reader

An Android RSS reader. Feeds and articles are synced from an account (Feedbin, Google Reader,
Miniflux, or a purely local account), read in an article view, and — as of the text-to-speech
work — optionally listened to.

## Language

### Reading

**Article**:
A single entry from a feed, with a title, a byline, and body markup.
_Avoid_: Entry, item, post

**Full Content**:
The article body as extracted from the original web page, used when a feed supplies only a
teaser. A per-feed preference, not a property of the article itself.
_Avoid_: Mercury content, extracted content, readability

**Enclosure**:
A media file the feed itself points at — most often a podcast episode. Supplied by the
publisher, never generated by Capy.
_Avoid_: Attachment, media

**Audio Enclosure**:
An **Enclosure** that is playable audio. The thing the floating player was originally built for.

### Listening

**Spoken Article**:
The audio rendering of one **Article**, synthesized on demand from its text. A Spoken Article is
not an **Enclosure** — the publisher had no part in it — but it is played by the same player,
appears in the same floating bar, and holds the same media session.
_Avoid_: TTS audio, narration, read-aloud

**Speakable Text**:
The prose of an **Article** with everything unlistenable removed — code blocks, tables, image
captions. What a **Speech Provider** is actually asked to say. It is derived from whatever the
reader is currently showing, so an article without **Full Content** yields only the teaser.
_Avoid_: Plain text, stripped content, transcript

**Passage**:
One contiguous run of **Speakable Text**, small enough that a **Speech Provider** will accept it
in a single request. A **Spoken Article** is a sequence of Passages played back to back; passage
boundaries are the only points at which playback can be interrupted or paid for separately.
_Avoid_: Chunk, segment, part

**Speech Provider**:
The third-party service that turns **Speakable Text** into audio. The user supplies their own
credentials; Capy never brokers access. Providers differ in what a **Passage** costs — an order
of magnitude between them — but none is cheap enough to fetch a Passage the reader may never
reach, so Capy fetches every Passage late whatever the provider.
_Avoid_: TTS engine, backend, vendor

**Voice**:
The named or identified speaker a **Speech Provider** uses. Distinct from playback speed, which
Capy applies locally to already-synthesized audio and which therefore costs nothing to change.

## Flagged ambiguities

**"Audio" is overloaded.** Before this work it meant "podcast episode from a feed." It now covers
two unrelated origins. Resolution: **Enclosure** is publisher-supplied, **Spoken Article** is
Capy-synthesized, and "audio" alone should only be used for the playback surface both share.
Note that the code represents a **Spoken Article** using the existing `AudioEnclosure` type — a
deliberate shortcut, and a place where the code and this glossary disagree.

**"Provider" collides with account sources.** Feedbin, Miniflux, and Google Reader are
**Sources**, not providers. **Speech Provider** always means the TTS service.

**"Read" collides.** An article is *read* (a sync state) or it is *read aloud* (playback).
Playback never changes the sync state.

## Example dialogue

> **Dev**: If someone taps Listen on a Verge item that's just a teaser, do we go get the real
> article first?
>
> **Domain expert**: No. The Speakable Text comes from whatever the reader is showing. If they
> want the whole thing they turn on Full Content for that feed first, same as if they were
> reading it.
>
> **Dev**: So they might pay for forty words.
>
> **Domain expert**: They might. But they'd have paid for the whole article without asking for it
> otherwise, and that's worse — a Passage isn't free.
>
> **Dev**: And if they change their mind halfway through, toggle Full Content, and hit Listen
> again?
>
> **Domain expert**: Different Speakable Text, so it's a different Spoken Article. Nothing we
> already synthesized applies — the Passages don't even line up.
>
> **Dev**: What if they just switch Voice?
>
> **Domain expert**: Also a different Spoken Article. Same text, different speaker. Changing
> *speed*, though, is free — that's us, not the Speech Provider.
